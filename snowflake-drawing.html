✏️ How to Draw a Snowflake: Step by Step

One step per box — draw each new part in a different color so kids can see exactly what changed.

Step 1: The Center Dot

Draw a small dot in the middle of your paper. Everything grows from here.

Step 2: Six Lines

Draw 6 straight lines radiating out from the dot, like a starburst or asterisk.

Step 3: Small Branches

On each line, draw two small branches near the outer end, like a tiny V.

Step 4: More Branches

Add two more branches halfway down each line — smaller than the first ones.

Step 5: Pretty Details

Add tiny dots, diamonds, or circles at the line tips and center.

Step 6: Color It In

Color it icy blue on white, or white on a blue background — real snowflake style!

What You'll Need to Draw a Snowflake

Light blue or white on dark blue paper — this is one tutorial where colored paper genuinely matters. White crayons are invisible on white paper, so grab dark blue or black construction paper. A white crayon or oil pastel with good coverage works best; regular crayons can look scratchy. Silver or glitter crayons make it magical.

What They're Learning

Snowflakes are all about symmetry — six identical arms radiating from a center point. This is the clearest introduction to radial symmetry a young kid can get, and they feel the satisfaction when all six arms match. The small branch details on each arm are precise work that really sharpens fine motor control.

Make It Yours

No two real snowflakes are alike, so challenge kids to make each arm slightly different — different branch patterns, different lengths. Draw a blizzard with snowflakes of all sizes falling, or one giant detailed snowflake as a winter decoration. Cutting the paper snowflake out after drawing it turns it into a window decoration.

❓ Snowflake Drawing FAQs

What is the easiest way for a kid to draw a snowflake?

Draw a dot, then six straight lines radiating out like an asterisk. Add small V-shaped branches on each line — bigger near the tips, smaller toward the center. Finish with dots or diamonds. Symmetry does the beauty work for you.

What age can kids start learning to draw a snowflake?

Most kids can manage the radiating lines around age 5 with help spacing them, and the full snowflake solo by 6 or 7. Younger kids can trace the asterisk and add dots.

What supplies do we need to draw a snowflake?

Paper and crayons or markers — icy blue and white are perfect. Try white crayon on blue paper for a magical reveal effect. The free printable PDF lays out all six steps one per box.
