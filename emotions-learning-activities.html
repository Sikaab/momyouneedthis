Emotional learning does not have to be complicated. These printable activities give young children simple, playful ways to explore feelings — what they are called, what causes them, and what helps when they show up.

💛 Learn About Emotions

Children circle the face that matches how they feel today — happy, sad, angry, scared, silly, or tired — then draw their own mouth in the big “Feeling Mirror” and finish the sentence “Today I feel ___ because ___.” The check-in keeps language simple so ages 2 to 5 can follow along with a grown-up.

🔍 Narrate Feelings During The Day

The printable is a starting point — real learning happens in daily moments. Narrate what you see: “You look proud of that tower!” or “I feel frustrated this jar won’t open.” Hearing feeling words in context cements them faster than any worksheet alone.

💡 Why Emotional Learning Starts Early

Emotional vocabulary is linked to better self-control, stronger friendships, and easier transitions at preschool. Kids who can name their feelings are better equipped to handle them — and to understand the feelings of the people around them.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What will my child learn from these emotions activities?

The names of common feelings — happy, sad, angry, scared, silly, and tired — and how to spot them in faces, including their own. It is a broad, gentle introduction designed for ages 2 to 5.

Are these printables really free?

Yes — every printable on MomYouNeedThis is 100% free to download and print. No signup, no payment, just tap the download button.

How often should we do emotions activities?

A little goes a long way. One short session a week plus naming feelings in everyday moments is plenty. The goal is steady, low-pressure exposure — not a crash course.
