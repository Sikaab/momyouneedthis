🔍 Make A Feely Box

Cut a hand-hole in a shoebox, drop in familiar objects — a spoon, a cotton ball, a pinecone — and let your child guess by touch alone. Ask “what does it feel like?” to pull out describing words like bumpy, fuzzy, and cold.

💡 Why Touch Exploration Matters

Feeling different textures builds sensory processing skills and descriptive vocabulary at the same time. Words like rough, smooth, squishy, and prickly are learned best through real hands-on experience — exactly what these activities provide.
