✏️ What Are Pre-Writing Strokes?

Children learn that every letter is made of a few simple strokes — straight lines, slanted lines, curves, and circles. Practicing strokes separately, like learning notes before playing a song, makes writing letters much easier later.

🎵 Stroke Song Game

Name each stroke as you practice — 'down-down-down!' for vertical lines, 'round-and-round!' for circles. Chanting turns stroke practice into a rhythm game little hands love.

💡 Why Practice Pre-Writing?

Research shows children who master basic strokes before attempting letters write more confidently and with less frustration. Strokes isolate the hard part — controlling the pencil — from the harder part of remembering letter shapes.

❓ Pre-Writing FAQs

What are pre-writing strokes?

Pre-writing strokes are the basic pencil marks — vertical, horizontal, and diagonal lines, curves, circles, and crosses — that combine to form every letter and number. Mastering them first makes learning to write much smoother.

How do I use these pre-writing worksheets with my child?

Print the worksheet and work through it in short, playful sessions. Let your child use crayons, markers, or even a finger in shaving cream for sensory fun — the goal is joyful practice, not perfection.
