🗣️ Stretch The First Sound

Work in passes. First pass: say each picture's name and stretch its opening — d-d-dog is the model. Second pass: draw lines only to the pictures that tapped with a buzz. Two passes keep the listening and the pencil work separate, which makes both better.

🔊 Matching The D Sound

A line on this page is a claim: “this word starts like dog.” Your child tests each picture against the tap — tongue flicking behind the top teeth, voice humming — and only the true taps get connected to the letter D. Pictures that merely show things dogs like stay unconnected, and leaving them out takes as much listening as matching the right ones.

The reference trick works beautifully here: dog is a word most children can say and hear clearly. “Does it start like dog?” converts every new picture into a comparison a preschooler can actually perform, instead of a sound they have to recall from nowhere.

D's impostor is t — the same tap with the voice switched off. The throat test settles every dispute: fingers on the throat, say the word slowly. Buzz means d and the line gets drawn; silence means t and it does not.
