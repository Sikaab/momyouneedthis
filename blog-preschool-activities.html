15 Learning Activities You Can Do Without Buying Anything

You don't need a house full of educational toys to give your preschooler meaningful learning opportunities. Here are 15 simple activities you can do with things you probably already have at home — plus free activities from MomYouNeedThis that you can print and use anytime.

💗 MomYouNeedThis • Free Preschool Activity Guide
Learning doesn't have to cost money.

Preschoolers learn through everyday experiences: talking, sorting, counting, drawing, moving, pretending, matching and exploring.

Before buying another educational toy or activity kit, look around your home. You may already have everything you need for a surprisingly good learning activity.

💡 The Quick Answer

The best preschool learning activities use what you already have: a colour hunt around the house, sorting socks, counting snacks, drawing stories, building toy patterns and nature counting walks. Five or ten minutes of playful learning is enough — follow your child's interest and keep it fun.

The best preschool learning activities don't have to be complicated. A cardboard box, a few pieces of paper, household objects or even a walk outside can become an opportunity to practise important skills.

The goal isn't to turn every moment into a lesson. It's simply to give your child opportunities to explore skills through play.

1. 🎨 How Do You Play A Colour Hunt?

Pick a colour and challenge your preschooler to find objects around the house that match it.

For example:

🔴 Find something red.
🔵 Find something blue.
🟡 Find something yellow.

Once they understand the game, let your child choose the colour.

This simple activity encourages colour recognition, vocabulary, observation and categorization.

2. 🧦 What Can We Sort Around The House?

Collect a small group of safe household objects and ask your child to sort them.

You could sort objects by:

  • Colour
  • Size
  • Shape
  • Type
  • Texture

You don't need special sorting toys. Try socks, plastic containers, spoons, blocks, toy animals or other safe objects you already have.

Make it harder:

Ask your child why they put each object into a particular group.

3. 🔺 How Does A Shape Hunt Work?

Choose a shape and look around the house for examples.

Ask:

“Can you find something shaped like a circle?”

You might discover a plate, a clock, a lid or a ball.

Then try another shape.

4. ✏️ What Pre-Writing Patterns Should We Practise?

Before children learn to write letters, they benefit from practising the movements involved in writing.

Draw simple patterns such as:

  • Vertical lines
  • Horizontal lines
  • Zigzags
  • Curves
  • Circles
  • Simple repeating patterns

Your child can trace your lines, copy them or create their own patterns.

5. 🔢 How Can I Make A Homemade Counting Game?

Grab a few household objects and practise counting together.

You could use:

  • Socks
  • Plastic cups
  • Spoons
  • Toy animals
  • Blocks
  • Pasta

Put five objects on the table and ask your child to count them. Then add one more.

5 objects + 1 more → How many now?

Keep the numbers appropriate for your child's current ability.

6. ⭐ How Do You Make A Shape Matching Game?

Draw simple shapes on pieces of paper and create matching pairs.

Mix them up and ask your child to find the matching shapes.

As your child gets better at it, add more shapes or introduce different sizes.

7. 🌿 How Can A Nature Walk Become A Counting Game?

Take a walk outside and turn it into a simple counting game.

Ask your preschooler to count things you see along the way:

  • Cars
  • Trees
  • Flowers
  • Birds
  • Benches
  • Dogs

You can also compare what you see:

“Which tree looks taller?”

This combines counting, observation, comparison and vocabulary without feeling like formal schoolwork.

8. 🔤 What Is A Letter Hunt?

Choose one letter and look for it around the house.

You can search through:

  • Food packages
  • Books
  • Magazines
  • Labels
  • Signs

Start with a letter your child already recognizes and gradually introduce new ones.

Wondering about timing? See when kids start learning their letters .

Keep it playful.

You don't need to test your child on every letter. Point out letters naturally and celebrate when they recognize one.

9. 🦕 How Can We Draw A Story Together?

Give your child a piece of paper and start a simple story.

For example:

“Once there was a little dinosaur who found something mysterious...”

Ask your child what happens next and let them draw the story.

There doesn't need to be a perfect picture or a finished story. The activity encourages imagination, vocabulary, communication and storytelling.

10. 🧩 How Do You Build A Pattern With Toys?

Use toys, blocks, crayons or household objects to create a simple pattern.

Red → Blue → Red → Blue

Ask your child:

“What comes next?”

Once they understand a simple repeating pattern, make the pattern longer or introduce three different objects.

12. 🍎 How Do You Play 'Which One Doesn't Belong'?

Place three or four objects together and make one of them different.

For example:

Ask your child which one doesn't belong and why.

There can sometimes be more than one reasonable answer. That's actually part of the fun.

You're encouraging your child to observe characteristics, compare objects and explain their thinking.

13. 🃏 How Do You Make A Homemade Memory Game?

Draw simple pictures or shapes on pairs of pieces of paper.

Place them face down and take turns flipping two cards.

If the pictures match, keep the pair. If they don't, turn them back over.

You can start with only three or four pairs so the game isn't too difficult.

Learning without realizing it.

Memory games encourage attention, visual memory, turn-taking and matching.

14. 🍪 How Can Snack Time Become A Learning Activity?

You don't have to prepare a special activity. Everyday routines can become learning opportunities.

During snack time, ask questions like:

  • “How many crackers do you have?”
  • “Which one is bigger?”
  • “Can you put three on your plate?”
  • “Which colour do you have more of?”
  • “What happens if we add one more?”

You're practising counting, comparison, language and simple math while doing something you already needed to do.

15. 💗 What Is A 'Teach Me Something' Moment?

This one costs absolutely nothing.

Ask your child to teach you something they know.

“Can you teach Mommy how to do it?”

They might show you how to build something, explain a game, name animals, tell you about a favourite toy or demonstrate something they've learned.

Let them take the lead.

This encourages communication, confidence, memory and expressive language — and you may be surprised by how much they know.

💗 Do I Need To Do All 15 Activities?

You don't need to turn your home into a preschool classroom.

Pick one activity that sounds fun and try it. If your child isn't interested, that's okay too.

Learning happens through everyday conversations, play, movement, exploration and repetition.

Follow your child's interest. Keep it playful. 💗

Quick note: I'm a mom, not a child development specialist, and these are play ideas — not milestones to hit. If you have concerns about your child's development, check with your pediatrician. 💗

🌈

Some Of The Best Learning Happens With What You Already Have

You don't need expensive toys, elaborate crafts or a perfectly planned schedule to support your preschooler's development.

A walk around the neighbourhood can become a counting game. A handful of socks can become a sorting activity. A piece of paper can become a drawing lesson. And a conversation can become a language activity.

The most important thing is giving your child opportunities to explore, ask questions, try things and spend time learning with you.

Simple can be enough. 💗

🎨 Preschool Activity Questions, Answered

What are the best learning activities for preschoolers at home?

Simple play-based activities work best: colour hunts, sorting household objects, shape hunts, pre-writing patterns, homemade counting games, shape matching, nature counting walks, letter hunts, drawing stories together, building toy patterns, drawing shapes, “which one doesn't belong”, memory games, snack-time learning, and “teach me something” moments. You don't need special toys — things you already have at home are enough.

How long should a preschool learning activity last?

Five or ten minutes of playful learning can be enough. Follow your child's interest and let the activity naturally end when they've had enough. You don't need to do all 15 activities or turn your home into a preschool classroom.

Do I need to buy educational toys for my preschooler?

No. Preschoolers learn through everyday experiences: talking, sorting, counting, drawing, moving, pretending, matching and exploring. Before buying another educational toy or activity kit, look around your home — you may already have everything you need.

How can I practise pre-writing skills with my preschooler?

Before children learn to write letters, they benefit from practising the movements involved in writing: vertical lines, horizontal lines, zigzags, curves, circles and simple repeating patterns. Your child can trace your lines, copy them or create their own patterns.
