Keeping kids safe around strangers starts with simple, clear ideas. These printable activities teach young children to recognize tricky situations and always tell a trusted grown-up — in language made for ages 2 to 5.

🛡️ Learn About Stranger Safety

Children sort picture scenarios into “safe” and “tell a grown-up” groups, learn that tricky people may break safety rules (asking a child for help, asking them to keep secrets), and practice the simple response: say no, run to a trusted grown-up, and tell. The focus is on behaviors, not appearances.

🔍 Name The Trusted Grown-Ups

Together, list the grown-ups your child can always go to — you, grandparents, teachers, specific neighbors. Write or draw them on the printable. When kids can name their safe people, the abstract rule “tell someone” becomes concrete and usable.

💡 Why “Tricky” Beats “Stranger”

Safety educators recommend teaching children about tricky behaviors rather than stranger appearances, since most harm comes from someone the child knows. Focusing on rules — “grown-ups don’t ask kids for help” — gives children a practical tool instead of vague fear.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What should preschoolers know about stranger safety?

Simple, behavior-based rules: tricky people break safety rules (ask kids for help, ask them to keep secrets), and the response is always the same — say no, get away, and tell a trusted grown-up. These activities teach exactly that.

Are these printables really free?

Yes — every printable on MomYouNeedThis is 100% free to download and print. No signup, no payment, just tap the download button.

How do I talk about this without scaring my child?

Keep it brief, calm, and empowering — like learning to look both ways. Focus on what TO do (tell a trusted grown-up) rather than scary what-ifs, and let your matter-of-fact tone set the mood.
