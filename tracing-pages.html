Which Tracing Page Should My Child Start With?

New tracers do best in this order — each step builds the hand control the next one needs:

  1. Lines and paths first. Straight lines and simple paths from Line Tracing Practice and Pre-Writing Practice teach the pencil to move on purpose.
  2. Curves next. Curve & Path Tracing adds loops and waves — the movements inside most letters.
  3. Then shapes. Shape Tracing Pages combine lines and curves into circles, squares and triangles.
  4. Letters and numbers last. Alphabet Tracing and Number Tracing turn all that control into real writing.

There is no schedule to keep — stay on a step while it is fun, and move on when the pages start to feel easy.

Why Tracing Practice Helps Kids Learn

Tracing activities help children strengthen fine motor skills, hand-eye coordination, pencil control, and early handwriting abilities. They provide a gentle introduction to writing before children begin forming letters and numbers independently.
