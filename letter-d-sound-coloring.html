🔎 Say The Words Together

Before a single crayon moves, tour the page and say every picture's name together. Keep dog in mind as the reference: if a name opens with the same tongue tap, it is a coloring candidate; if it opens with anything else, it waits. Naming first turns coloring time into listening time.

The uncolored pictures are not mistakes — they are the proof the listening worked. Point at one together and say its name: no tap, no color. If one of them taps quietly — a t — the throat test (buzz or no buzz) explains exactly why it stayed white.

🎨 Which Pictures Earn Their Color? The D Rule

Dog, duck and drum earn their color on this page because they open with a tap — the tongue flicking behind the top teeth. That is the whole rule. A picture of something a dog would love does not get colored for being lovable; the ears, not the eyes, hand out the crayons here.

D's sound-alike is t: same tap, no voice. A picture whose name opens with a quiet tap is a t, and it stays white. The throat test settles it — fingers on the throat, say the word: buzz means d, silence means t. It is the most reliable trick in this whole set of pages.

When every tap-picture is colored, count the gallery and tap the table once for each: dog, duck, drum. Then let your child color the leftovers however they like — the listening is done, and the page can end as a treat.
