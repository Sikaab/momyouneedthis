How To Use This Printable

  1. Download and print the weather and seasons worksheet on letter-size paper.
  2. Start with today — name today's weather and the season it is visiting, and mark both on the page.
  3. Work the seasons in order — spring, summer, autumn, winter — saying at each one what changes: the weather, the trees, the clothes, the games.
  4. Keep the worksheet near the door — a week of morning checks (weather today? still our season?) turns one worksheet into a daily science habit.

Why Learn About Weather & Seasons?

Seasons are a child's first experience of large-scale time: a cycle too big to see in a day, visible only by watching and remembering. Learning them builds the patience of observation — the understanding that the bare tree is not dead but mid-story — alongside a working vocabulary for weather, clothing, plants and holidays.

It also builds prediction, the scientist's core move. A child who knows autumn follows summer can anticipate the leaves, the jackets and the first frost — and a child who watches weather daily learns that forecasts are guesses checked against the sky, theirs to verify every morning.

🍂 The Four Seasons, Side By Side

Spring — the warming. Rain more than snow, buds and first flowers, baby animals, puddles worth engineering. Clothes: layers and rain boots. The season of starting.

Summer — the heat. Long days, short nights, thunderstorms, gardens at full speed, water play. Clothes: the fewest of the year, plus hat and sunscreen. The season of outside.

Autumn (fall) — the cooling. Leaves stop making green and show their hidden colors, then drop; harvests come in; animals cache, migrate or grow winter coats. Clothes: sweaters return. The season of preparing.

Winter — the cold. Short days, bare trees, snow where the climate allows, breath visible in the air. Clothes: the full armor — coat, hat, mittens, boots. The season of resting, for trees and gardens and, traditionally, for everyone's plans.

One globe-sized footnote children love: when it is winter here, it is summer in Australia — the seasons flip on the other half of the Earth, because seasons come from the planet's tilt toward and away from the sun, not from the calendar. December is beach weather somewhere, and that somewhere is a real place with penguins nearby wearing no coats at all — they have their own.

Is this weather seasons worksheet printable really free?

Yes — this weather and seasons worksheet is 100% free to download and print at home. No signup and no payment needed. Print four copies if your child wants one per season to fill in as the year actually turns — the set becomes a year-long record.

What ages is this printable best for?

It is made for toddlers and preschoolers, ages 2–5. Little ones (2–3) sort by today's weather and clothing; older preschoolers (4–5) can hold the whole four-season cycle, order it, and explain what changes at each turn. The morning-check routine works at every age in that range.
