✏️ How to Draw a Reindeer: Step by Step

One step per box — draw each new part in a different color so kids can see exactly what changed.

Step 1: The Head

Draw an oval in the upper-left area of your paper. This is your reindeer's head, seen from the side. Leave room to the right for the body.

Step 2: The Antlers

Draw branching antlers on top of the head — a main branch with 2 or 3 smaller branches splitting off each side, like tiny trees.

Step 3: The Face

Add two dot eyes, a big round red nose in the middle, and a smile underneath. The red nose is what makes it a very special reindeer.

Step 4: The Body

Draw a long horizontal oval for the body — like a horse's torso, stretched out sideways. Connect it to the head with two short curved neck lines.

Step 5: The Legs and Tail

Add four legs under the body — two near the front, two near the back, like a horse standing. Add a short fluffy tail at the rear.

Step 6: Color It In

Color your reindeer! Brown body, tan antlers, and of course a bright red nose.

🦌 Reindeer Games Challenge

Draw your reindeer, then draw a second one right next to it — but change one thing. Different antler shapes, a bigger nose, spots instead of plain. Comparing two drawings side by side builds observation skills, and kids love spotting the differences in their own work.

What You'll Need to Draw a Reindeer

Grab crayons or markers — brown for the body, a lighter tan for antlers, and red for the all-important nose. Black works for eyes and hooves. Standard printer paper is fine.

What They're Learning

This reindeer tutorial practices ovals in two sizes — comparing the small head oval to the big body oval teaches proportional thinking. The branching antlers are the trickiest part and build careful line control, since each branch has to connect. Placing the face features in the center of the oval builds spatial centering skills.

Make It Yours

Once the basic reindeer is done, try these twists: add white spots like a fawn, draw a bell on a collar around its neck, or give it a scarf for a winter look. Kids can also draw the whole sleigh team — nine reindeer gets crowded, so try three in a row. A snowy background with falling snow dots completes the scene.

💡 Reindeer Drawing Tips for Parents

The reindeer body is the step that trips kids up — it's a long horizontal oval, like a horse lying sideways, not a round ball. If your child draws a circle, gently remind them: "reindeer bodies are long, like a horse." The antlers go on top of the head, angled outward like a V, with the bases touching the head. Antlers on the sides look like ears — on top, they look like antlers.

Two things that help with every reindeer drawing attempt: let them pick the colors (a blue reindeer is still a reindeer), and don't fix their drawing. If they ask for help, draw on a separate piece of paper, not on theirs. Kids who feel ownership of their drawing come back for the next one; kids who feel corrected stop trying. The goal is a reindeer they're proud of, not a perfect one.

🦌 Reindeer in the Real World

Real reindeer live in the far north, and both males and females grow antlers — reindeer are the only deer where the females grow them too. They shed their antlers and grow a fresh, bigger set every year. The antlers in this drawing branch like tiny trees because real ones grow that way: new points split off the main beam as the antler gets bigger.

A reindeer's nose is a built-in heater, packed with tiny blood vessels that warm freezing air before it reaches the lungs — which is why a real reindeer's nose can look pink in the cold. Their hooves click as they walk, because tendons snap over the bone, so a herd announces itself before you see it. In North America, wild reindeer are called caribou.

✋ Warm-Up: Ovals and Antler Branches

The reindeer is built from ovals and branches, so practise both on scrap paper first. Draw ovals in two sizes: small ones for the head, long stretched ones for the body. Then the signature move — branches. Draw one main line, then split two or three smaller lines off it, like a tiny tree. Do a whole row of them.

Branches are the step kids rush, and thirty seconds of practice trees makes step 2 feel easy. Finish with a few big confident circles: one of them is about to become the most famous nose in the world, and it deserves a steady hand.

📖 Give Your Reindeer a Story

Every reindeer on the team has a name and a job, so give this one both. Is it the famous red-nosed leader, a brand-new recruit on its first Christmas Eve, or the one who carries the snacks? Have your child dictate a sentence or two for under the drawing — name, job, and tonight's destination — and sign it like the team's official record-keeper.

One reindeer with a story is rarely the end of it. A teammate can join at the next drawing session, with its own name and its own job, until a whole team stands in a row across the fridge. Stories are how single drawings turn into a world your child wants to keep adding to.

🦌 Reindeer Drawing Step by Step: Fixes for Wobbly Reindeer

Antlers cause the most wobbles, in two predictable ways. If they sprout from the sides of the head, they read as ears — antlers belong on top, angled outward like a V, with their bases touching the head. If a finished antler floats just above the head, don't erase it: extend each branch's base down until it lands. A connected antler is a believable antler, even a wobbly one. Two or three branches per side is plenty — a thicket of ten turns antlers into a bush.

The body is the second wobble. A round ball body makes a reindeer that looks like it is sitting down. The rescue is to stretch, not restart: draw the neck lines long, place two legs near the front and two near the back, and let a long ground line underneath sell the horse-like length. If a leg lands in the wrong spot, bend it — a reindeer kneeling in the snow is a story, not a mistake.

Finally, the face. The red nose does the recognising, so it wants to sit in the middle of the head oval with the smile tucked underneath it. A nose that drifted to the edge turns the reindeer in profile — which is fine, and very deer-like — but if your child wanted the famous front-facing Rudolph look, a second, bigger nose circle drawn over the first, right in the centre, fixes it in one move. Colour it bright red and let it be the reward it was saved up to be.

Start with an oval for the head and add branching antlers on top. Give it dot eyes and a big red nose, then add a long horse-like body and four legs. Ovals and branches do all the work.
