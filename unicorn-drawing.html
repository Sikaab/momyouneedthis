The unicorn is the most requested drawing in kid history, and the secret is that it's just a pretty horse head plus three magic ingredients: a horn, a rainbow mane, and closed happy eyes. Nail those three and the magic takes care of itself.

✏️ How to Draw a Unicorn: Step by Step

One step per box — draw each new part in a different color so kids can see exactly what changed.

Step 1: Step 1: The Horse Head

Draw a long rounded shape like a stretched teardrop or a sock — this is the unicorn's elegant head.

Step 2: Step 2: The Magic Horn

Draw a tall triangle on the forehead with spiral lines wrapped around it — the horn!

Step 3: Step 3: The Flowing Mane

Draw big swooping curves along the back of the head for a flowing mane.

Step 4: Step 4: The Ears

Draw two leaf-shaped ears on top of the head, peeking through the mane.

Step 5: Step 5: The Happy Face

Add closed happy eyes (two curved lines), a tiny nose, and a gentle smile.

Step 6: Step 6: Rainbow Magic

Color the mane and horn in rainbow stripes — pink, purple, blue, yellow. Pure magic!

🦄 Rainbow Mane Salon

Draw the unicorn head three times and give each one a different mane style — rainbow stripes, starry night, cotton-candy swirls. It's the same head three times, but kids treat each one as a new character with a name. The mane is where their personality lands on the page.

What You'll Need to Draw a Unicorn

White or very light pink for the body (leave mostly uncolored on white paper, or use light gray paper). Rainbow colors for the mane and tail — this is the place to use every crayon in the box. Gold or yellow for the horn. Sparkle stickers or glitter crayons take it to the next level if you have them.

What They're Learning

Unicorns build on the horse/dog body basics but add the horn (a triangle with spiral lines) — combining a familiar shape with a new detail. The rainbow mane is color-planning practice: kids decide the order of colors before starting. The small sparkles and stars around it are fun detail work that rewards a light, careful touch.

Make It Yours

Give it a rainbow arcing overhead, stars and clouds around it, or a whole field of flowers. A baby unicorn (chubbier, bigger eyes) next to a parent is adorable. Try a galaxy unicorn with dark blue body and star speckles, or an ice unicorn in blues and whites. A unicorn with a braided mane (three wavy lines twisted) is a fun challenge.

What is the easiest way for a kid to draw a unicorn?

Draw a long teardrop shape for the horse-like head. Add a tall triangle horn with spiral lines on the forehead, swooping curves for the mane, leaf-shaped ears, and closed happy eyes. Color the mane rainbow — the horn and rainbow mane are what make it a unicorn.

Most kids can manage the head and horn around age 5 with help, and the full unicorn solo by 6 or 7. Younger kids can trace the head and pour their energy into the rainbow mane.
