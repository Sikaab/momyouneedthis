✏️ Draw Your Cat in 6 Easy Steps

One step per box — this step by step cat drawing shows each new part in a different color so kids can see exactly what changed.

Step 1: The Head

Draw a big circle in the middle of your paper. This round shape becomes your cat's head.

Step 2: The Ears

Add two triangle ears on top of the circle. Pointy ears make it look extra cat-like.

Step 3: The Face

Draw two dot eyes, a tiny triangle nose, a w-shaped mouth, and three whisker lines on each cheek. The whiskers are the best part!

Step 4: The Body

Draw an oval below the head for the body — a little smaller than the head circle. Let it overlap the head slightly.

Step 5: The Legs and Tail

Add four legs under the body — two in front, two peeking out behind — and a long curvy tail swooping out to one side. A big curl makes a happy cat.

Step 6: Color It In

Color your cat! Orange with stripes is classic, but gray, black, or even purple cats are all great.

🐱 Copycat Challenge

Draw your cat twice — once sitting and once with the tail curled the other way. Then draw a real cat (or a stuffed animal) next to the cartoon one and compare. Which ears are pointier? Which tail is curlier? Drawing the same cat twice builds observation skills without feeling like practice.

What You'll Need to Draw a Cat

Grab a black crayon or marker for the outlines, plus whatever color your kid wants their cat to be — orange, gray, black, or something wild. Standard printer paper works fine. The whiskers are easier with a fine-tip marker if you have one, but crayons work too.

What They're Learning

This cat tutorial practices circles, ovals, and triangles — three core shapes in one drawing. The whiskers are straight-line practice that builds hand control, and placing the face features (two eyes, one nose, one mouth) teaches spatial awareness — everything has its spot. Following six steps in order builds sequencing skills too.

Make It Yours

Once the basic cat is done, try these twists: add stripes like a tiger cat, give it a bow tie or a collar, or draw it sleeping with closed curved-line eyes. Kids can also draw a whole cat family — big cats, tiny kittens, each one a different color. A fish bowl next to the cat tells a whole story.

💡 Cat Drawing Tips for Parents

The trickiest part of this cat drawing is getting all four legs in — two front, two behind. If your child forgets the back legs, point at the picture and ask "where are the other two legs?" rather than drawing them yourself. Kids remember steps they figure out better than steps they were shown. And if the tail ends up on the wrong side, leave it — a cat with a tail on either side is still a cat.

Two things that help with every cat drawing attempt: let them pick the colors (a blue cat is still a cat), and don't fix their drawing. If they ask for help, draw on a separate piece of paper, not on theirs. Kids who feel ownership of their drawing come back for the next one; kids who feel corrected stop trying. The goal is a cat they're proud of, not a perfect one.

🐱 Cats in the Real World

Real cats are champion sleepers: most cats sleep for well over half the day, saving their energy for dawn and dusk, when their wild cousins hunt. That is why the family cat holds its most important meetings at 5 a.m. Cats also see far better in dim light than people do, and their ears can swivel like little satellite dishes toward a sound.

A cat's whiskers are feelers, not decoration — they sense air movements and help the cat judge spaces in the dark. And while dogs wag and bark their feelings, cats talk quietly: a slow blink from across the room is a cat saying it trusts you. Try slow-blinking at the drawing when it is done; artists should bond with their subjects.

✋ Warm-Up: Ears, Whiskers, and Tails

Warm up with the cat's three signature lines. Triangles first: pairs of pointy ears, over and over, because a cat is often just a circle that grew ears. Then whisker fans — three quick light strokes from one point, each side. Finish with tail curves: long question-mark swoops lying down, curled tight and stretched lazy.

Warm-ups are where wobbly whiskers go to be free. In the real drawing, a whisker that doubles or crosses is a whisker with company — real cats' whiskers overlap constantly. Save the eraser for an ear that truly lands on the wrong side of the head.

🐱 Cat Drawing Easy Step by Step: Fixes for Wobbly Cats

The ears make the first call in any cat drawing easy step by step: triangles that come out too tall and sharp turn the cat into a fox. The fix is proportion, not precision — ears about a quarter of the head's height, with the tips softened, read as cat every time. If the ears are already drawn fox-long, lean in and add a cheek fluff zigzag at step 3; or accept the fox and draw this page's cat again beside it. Kids mind far less than adults do.

Whiskers cause the second tangle. Drawn too long or aimed upward, they cross the eyes and the face turns busy. Keep three whiskers per cheek, shorter than the ear is tall, angled gently downward like the cat is smiling. The little w mouth gets the same advice as the bunny page: practice it twice on scrap paper, or swap in a plain smile curve — the cat stays cute. And if the face ends up slightly lopsided, that is a cat mid-meow, not a mistake.

The tail is the last trap: attached too high on the body, it reads as a flag or a second ear. The tail grows from the back of the body, down low, and curves up at the tip — one long S lying along the ground line looks laziest and best. That is the pattern of a cat drawing easy step by step: every fix is a small adjustment to a shape already on the page. Color it orange with stripes, and a wobbly first cat becomes the practice run for the one they draw from memory.

🎀 How to Draw Kawaii Cat: The Extra-Cute Variation

When kids ask how to draw kawaii cat characters — the extra-cute style with the huge head and tiny body — they do not need a new tutorial. Kawaii is this page's same six steps with the proportions turned up. Draw the step 1 head circle much bigger, about half the whole cat, and shrink the step 4 body to a small oval tucked underneath. Ears stay triangles, but shorter and rounder at the tips, like the cat is still a kitten.

The face is where kawaii actually happens, at step 3. Swap the dot eyes for two tall ovals, and add a tiny white circle inside each one — that sparkle dot is the whole style in one move. Keep the nose and mouth as small as they can go, shorten the whiskers to two per cheek, and at coloring time add two pink ovals on the cheeks for blush. A small bow or star beside one ear finishes it. Learning how to draw kawaii cat versions of a tutorial they already know shows kids something bigger: a drawing style is a set of dials, and they are allowed to turn them.
