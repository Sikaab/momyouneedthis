📊 THE MOM SEARCH INDEX — WHAT MOMS GOOGLE, BY THE NUMBERS

The Mom Search Index What Moms Search For Most — and the Months They Search for It

We took the 2,050 real search terms in the MomYouNeedThis keyword bank — every coloring page, worksheet, chart and 2 a.m. schedule question in it — grouped them into the intents behind the searches, and read a full year of monthly volumes. What came back is a map of what moms actually need, and a calendar of when they need it. Some of it surprised us, too.

💗 MomYouNeedThis • Last updated: November 6, 2026

Quick Answer

Drawing is the #1 thing moms search for — drawing tutorials pull about 3× the searches of coloring pages (4,924,340 vs 1,648,970 searches a month, DataForSEO volumes, deduplicated). And mom searches run on a calendar: Halloween coloring searches spike 187× from their January floor to their October peak, Easter gets a six-week rocket, and potty training belongs to January.

At a Glance

  • Built from: A 2,050-term bank of real parent searches
  • Volume ruler: DataForSEO (Google Ads volumes, US, monthly)
  • Season window: October 2025 – September 2026, month by month
  • Biggest intent: Drawing tutorials — 4,924,340 searches/month
  • Sharpest spike: Easter coloring pages — ~279× from summer floor to March peak
  • Steadiest intent: Nap & sleep schedules — searched all year round

🗓️ THE SEASONAL CALENDAR

🗓️ The Seasonal Calendar: Mom Searches Run on a Schedule

Line the year up month by month (DataForSEO monthly series, October 2025 – September 2026) and parent demand turns out to have seasons as real as the weather. Five moments run the calendar:

September is the real start of Halloween. “Halloween coloring pages” sits at about 3,600 searches a month in January — then climbs to 33,100 in August and 165,000 in September, a full month before it peaks at 673,000 in October. A Halloween page published in October is a month late; September is the publish window, while moms are already searching.

Easter is a six-week rocket. “Easter coloring pages” is at 22,200 searches by February, peaks at 201,000 in March — about 279× its summer floor of 720 — and is back down to 1,600 by May. There is no long tail to catch: the window opens, spikes, and closes in about six weeks.

Potty training is a January intent. The potty training intent peaks in January at 72,100 searches a month, 4× its September low, and “potty training chart” jumps +123% from December (8,100) to January (18,100). New year, fresh start, same toddler — parents act on the resolution in January, not in the spring advice columns.

Tracing runs back-to-school, against the grain. While most of the bank fades after autumn, tracing searches peak in September (22,260 a month), with August second, and trough in December. It rises in exactly the months the wider bank falls — a school-year intent, not a holiday one. (Small base — 7 monthly series — so read the shape as directional.)

Nap & sleep never takes a month off. Sleep-schedule searches hold a narrow 32,500–42,240 a month band all year — a peak-to-trough swing of about +30%, the flattest of any intent measured, against far larger swings elsewhere. Babies do not check the calendar, so neither do the parents searching for their nap schedules. Sleep is the bank's one genuinely evergreen intent.

One honesty note before anyone quotes the swings: the DataForSEO monthly series tilts autumn-high and summer-low across almost everything, so intent-level swing percentages in this index are indicative, upper-bound readings. The single-term holiday spikes above — Halloween's 187×, Easter's ~279× — are far larger than that tilt and are reported as the measured findings they are. The Method & Sources section below has the full detail.

✏️ THE CURSIVE BLIND SPOT

✏️ The Cursive Blind Spot

Every index has a hole in it, and ours is cursive. The bank holds just 3 cursive terms — and the competitor-ranked measurement of the same search universe put the cursive intent at 591,200 searches a month (DataForSEO), larger than this bank's sight words, tracing, nap & sleep and potty training intents combined.

That gap says something real about how parent-search coverage gets built: banks like ours grow from the pages a site already publishes, so they over-cover what the site does and under-cover what it has not built yet. Parents did not stop searching for cursive practice — “cursive alphabet” and its family are right there in the competitor pool at volume. The searches were always there; the coverage was not. We are publishing the hole in our own data because an index that only flatters its source is not worth citing.

🔬 METHOD & SOURCES

🔬 Method & Sources

This index was built by MomYouNeedThis from data files already in hand — no new measurement was commissioned for it — and every figure on this page can be traced to the steps below.

  • The bank: 2,050 parent search terms, assembled in October 2026 from competitor-ranked keyword research in the toddler, preschool and early-elementary space.
  • The ruler: All volumes are DataForSEO (the Google Ads search-volume database, US, monthly averages) unless a figure says otherwise. DataForSEO held volumes for 1,208 of the 2,050 terms; unmeasured terms count as zero, so every total here is a floor.
  • Deduplication: Terms were machine-clustered into intents, then spelling and format variants inside an intent (the ~20 ways to write fall/autumn coloring, for example) were collapsed to one canonical form and counted once, at the head volume. Intent totals are deduplicated intent volumes — never sums of spellings.
  • Seasonality: The monthly series behind the calendar is the same DataForSEO data — 12 monthly values per term, October 2025 – September 2026.
  • The tilt caveat: The DataForSEO monthly series reads high in October–December and low in summer across nearly all intents, seasonal or not. Intent-level swing percentages are therefore labeled indicative, upper-bound figures. The holiday-term spikes (Halloween 187×, Easter ~279×) dwarf the tilt and are stated plainly as measured findings.
  • What we do not claim: Scavenger hunts carry no reliable seasonality in this data (only 1 of their 24 terms has a monthly series), so this page claims none for them. No Google Trends figures, estimates or modeled numbers are used anywhere in the index.
  • The cursive comparison uses a separate competitor-pool measurement dated October 20, 2026, on the same DataForSEO ruler; it is a statement about this bank's coverage, not about total market size.

FAQ

❓ Mom Search Index FAQ

What do moms search for most?

Drawing. In our 2,050-term keyword bank, drawing tutorials are the biggest parent-search intent at 4,924,340 searches a month (DataForSEO volumes, deduplicated) — about 3× the coloring pages intent at 1,648,970 a month. The single most-searched drawing term is “flower drawing” at 450,000 searches a month.

When do Halloween coloring page searches peak?

October — at 673,000 searches for “halloween coloring pages,” which is 187× the term's January floor of 3,600 (DataForSEO monthly series, October 2025 – September 2026). The useful detail is September: the term is already at 165,000 searches a month a full month before Halloween, so pages published in September catch the ramp instead of missing it.

Is potty training really a January thing?

In search data, yes. The potty training intent peaks in January at 72,100 searches a month — 4× its September low of 18,060 — and the head term “potty training chart” jumps +123% from December (8,100) to January (18,100) on the DataForSEO monthly series. New Year, fresh start, same toddler.

Which mom searches stay steady all year?

Nap and sleep schedules. Sleep-schedule searches stay in a narrow band of 32,500 to 42,240 searches a month across the whole year (DataForSEO) — the flattest intent we measured, because babies ignore the calendar. Most other intents swing far harder between their peak and trough months; those swing percentages are indicative upper bounds, as the Method & Sources section explains.

What is the Mom Search Index based on?

A bank of 2,050 real parent search terms, with monthly volumes from DataForSEO (the Google Ads volume database, US) covering October 2025 to September 2026. Spelling and format variants of the same search are collapsed and counted once, so every total is one intent's volume — never a sum of its spellings. DataForSEO had volumes for 1,208 of the 2,050 terms, so the totals are floors, not ceilings.

Why is cursive called a blind spot?

Because the demand is large and the coverage is nearly empty. Our bank holds just 3 cursive terms, while a competitor-ranked measurement put the cursive intent at 591,200 searches a month (DataForSEO) — larger than our bank's sight words, tracing, nap and potty intents combined. It is the clearest gap between what parents search for and what the bank was built to cover.
