🥚 All About the Oval

An oval looks like a circle that's been stretched — round with no corners, but longer one way than the other. Kids can find ovals in eggs, balloons, watermelons, mirrors, and racetracks.

💡 Why Color Ovals?

Ovals teach visual discrimination — the ability to notice small differences between similar shapes. This skill helps with letter recognition later, when children must tell apart look-alikes like O and Q or b and d.

What's the difference between a circle and an oval?

A circle is perfectly round — the same distance across in every direction. An oval is stretched longer one way. Show your child an egg next to a ball to make the difference clear.
