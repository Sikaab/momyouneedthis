🔍 Start A Kindness Jar

Keep a jar nearby and drop in a pom-pom every time someone in the family does something kind — no matter how small. When the jar is full, celebrate together. Kids love watching kindness add up, and the visual makes an abstract idea wonderfully real.

💡 Why Teaching Kindness Early Matters

Practicing kindness builds empathy, gratitude, and self-esteem all at once. Children who regularly notice and do kind acts develop stronger friendships and a more positive outlook — and prosocial behavior in early childhood is widely linked to wellbeing later on.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What are good kindness activities for toddlers?

Simple, concrete ones: sharing a toy, helping clean up, giving a hug, saying thank you, or handing someone a colored compliment card. This printable packages those ideas into a checklist and cards kids can actually use.

Are these printables really free?

Yes — every printable on MomYouNeedThis is 100% free to download and print. No signup, no payment, just tap the download button.

How do I teach kindness without lecturing?

Model it and notice it. Say “that was kind” when you see it, do kind acts together, and keep it playful. The printable gives you shared language — then your example does the heavy lifting.
