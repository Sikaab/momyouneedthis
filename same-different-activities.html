🔍 What Are Same & Different Activities?

Same and different activities show children rows of pictures and ask them to find which ones match — or which one does not belong. A page might show four apples and one banana, and your child circles the banana. These simple comparisons are the foundation of sorting, classifying, and logical thinking.

📋 How To Use These Activities

Print the worksheet and work through it together with a crayon or marker. For each row, ask "Which ones are the same?" and then "Which one is different?" Say the answers out loud together — naming builds vocabulary. If your child struggles, cover all but two pictures and compare just those.

🏠 Same & Different Around Your Home

Turn snack time into a learning game! Line up three crackers and one cookie and ask which is different. Sort laundry into same-color piles. Compare two stuffed animals: "Same ears? Different tails?" Everyday moments are full of comparing practice.

💡 Why Practice Same & Different?

Comparing is one of the first logic skills children develop, and it underlies almost everything in early learning — sorting shapes, recognizing letter differences, grouping numbers, and understanding categories. Same-and-different practice also grows descriptive vocabulary like big, small, round, and striped.

How can I help my toddler who finds it hard?

Start with just two pictures side by side and ask if they are the same or different. Use real objects like socks or blocks before moving to the printed page.
