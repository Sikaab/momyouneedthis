📝 How to Use These Drawing Tutorials

You don't need any special supplies — paper and crayons are enough. Here's the rhythm that works best:

  • Let them pick. Kids draw better when they chose the subject. Scroll the gallery together and let them tap the one that makes them grin.
  • One step at a time. Each tutorial shows the full drawing so far with the new part highlighted. Cover the later steps with your hand if your child gets ahead of themselves.
  • Different colors help. Have them draw each new step in a different crayon color — it makes "what changed" obvious and turns the tutorial into a color-by-step game.
  • Print it or don't. The steps work right on screen, but the printable PDF gives them a big sheet to practice on. Both are free.
  • Praise the trying. A lopsided fox is still a fox. The goal is confidence with a pencil, not perfection.

🧠 Why Step-by-Step Works for Kids

Young children think in wholes, not parts — "draw a dog" feels impossible because they see the finished animal, not the circle-head and oval-body hiding inside it. Breaking a drawing into 6 small steps teaches them to see shapes inside things, which is the actual skill behind all drawing.

These tutorials keep every step to a single new shape or detail. No step asks for two things at once, and no step assumes a skill from a different tutorial. A 3-year-old can do step 1 of any tutorial here; by step 6, they've drawn the whole thing themselves. This is preschool drawing at a pace little kids can actually follow.

The printable PDFs matter too. Drawing on a screen is fine, but pencil on paper builds the hand strength kids need for writing later. That's why every tutorial in this gallery has its own printable — drawing practice doubles as pre-writing practice.

🐾 Animals: The Most-Loved Drawing Subject

Animals are the most-requested drawing subject in our house, and easy how to draw animals tutorials are the ones kids actually finish. 🐰 Start With the Face — That Is Where the Cute Lives

If your child wants to learn how to draw cute animals, start with the face. Big round eyes, a tiny nose, and a small smile are what make an animal look cute — and they are also the easiest parts to draw. When you teach how to draw cute animals, the face does most of the work. The body can be one simple oval and the drawing still reads as adorable.

The best order for how to draw cute animals is: face first, then body, then ears and tail. Kids get the cute payoff early, which keeps them going through the rest of the steps. A bunny with a cute face is already a win at step two — the ears and body are just bonus.

Once your child knows how to draw cute animals, they will start inventing their own. A cat with bunny ears. A frog wearing a crown. That playful mixing is the goal — it means they understand the shapes well enough to play with them.

🦊 Less Is More: The Simple-Animal Rule

Learning how to draw simple animals is about subtraction, not addition. Take away the fur texture, the whiskers, the claws — and what is left is the animal in its simplest form. That is what makes how to draw simple animals so good for young kids: there is nothing fiddly to get wrong.

Every how to draw simple animals tutorial on this page follows the same rule: six steps, basic shapes only. A fox is a triangle-eared oval. A turtle is a dome with four legs. A penguin is two ovals and a beak. When kids learn how to draw simple animals this way, they build a mental library of shapes they can reuse for any animal.

Simple does not mean boring. A simple fox with a big bushy tail is still a fox, and kids know it. The simplicity is what makes the drawing feel achievable — and achievable drawings get finished, displayed, and drawn again.
