TODDLER & PRESCHOOL LEARNING

Easy Learning Activities for Toddlers & Preschoolers

You don't need expensive toys, complicated crafts or a perfect classroom at home. These simple activities turn everyday moments into opportunities to learn, explore and practise important skills.

KEEP IT SIMPLE

Learning can happen with what you already have

Toddlers and preschoolers learn through repetition, movement, conversation, exploration and play. A few minutes of intentional activity can be more useful than a shelf full of educational products.

🔤
LANGUAGE

Letters & sounds

Point out letters on books, signs, packaging and activity pages. Talk about the sounds you hear in familiar words.

🔢
MATH

Counting everywhere

Count steps, toys, snacks, blocks or anything else your child is interested in. Everyday counting is easy to repeat.

🎨
CREATIVITY

Draw, colour & create

Give your child paper and simple colouring materials and let them experiment with lines, shapes, colours and pictures.

ACTIVITY 01

Letter hunt around the house

Pick one letter and see how many times your child can find it around the house. Look through books, food packages, labels, signs or their own name.

🔎
YOU NEED

Just the environment around you

No special supplies are required. Choose a letter your child already recognizes or introduce a new one.

👀
ASK

"Can you find an A?"

Let your child point, touch or bring you examples. Celebrate the search rather than worrying about getting every answer right.

⭐
EXTEND IT

Find the sound too

Once your child is familiar with the letter, talk about the sound it can represent in simple familiar words.

ACTIVITY 02

Count everything

Turn ordinary routines into little math games by counting objects together.

🧸
TOYS

Count the toys

Put a few toys in a row and count them together. Then add one more and ask what changed.

🪜
MOVEMENT

Count steps

Count steps while walking upstairs, across a room or outside. Movement makes counting feel like part of the game.

🍓
MEALTIMES

Count snacks

Count pieces of fruit, crackers or other foods during snack preparation or mealtime.

ACTIVITY 03

Colour sorting game

Gather a few safe objects in different colours and ask your child to group them together.

🔴
START

Begin with two colours

A small number of categories makes it easier for younger toddlers to understand what you are asking.

🟡
TALK

Name each colour

Say the colour as your child sorts. Repetition helps build vocabulary and recognition.

🟢
EXTEND IT

Add more categories

Older preschoolers can sort by more than colour, such as size, shape or type of object.

ACTIVITY 04

Shape hunt

Look around your home or neighbourhood for circles, squares, rectangles and triangles.

⭕
CIRCLES

Find round objects

Look for plates, wheels, lids, clocks or anything else with a circular shape.

⬜
SQUARES

Spot four equal sides

Ask your child what they notice about a square and compare it with other shapes.

🔺
TRIANGLES

Look for three sides

Search for triangles around the house, in books, on signs or outside.

ACTIVITY 05

Name practice with everyday materials

Help your child become familiar with the letters in their name using paper, blocks, magnets or even objects arranged on the floor.

✏️
WRITE

Trace their name

Write their name in large letters and let them trace over it at their own pace.

🧩
BUILD

Build the letters

Use blocks, craft sticks or other safe materials to create simple letter shapes.

💬
TALK

Say the letters together

Point to each letter and say its name. For older preschoolers, you can also talk about the sounds.

ACTIVITY 06

DIY matching game

Draw simple pictures or shapes on small pieces of paper and make matching pairs.

🃏
MAKE

Create simple pairs

Draw two circles, two stars, two hearts or pairs of familiar objects.

🧠
PLAY

Find the match

Mix the cards and ask your child to find the matching pairs.

🔄
CHANGE IT

Increase the challenge

Add more pairs or use pictures that are similar but not identical for older preschoolers.

ACTIVITY 07

Build a pattern

Patterns are an easy way to introduce early math thinking without making the activity feel like a lesson.

🔴
START

Red, blue, red, blue

Use toys, blocks, socks or other safe objects to make a simple repeating pattern.

❓
ASK

"What comes next?"

Pause before the next item and let your child predict what should come next.

🌈
EXTEND

Make it more complicated

Older preschoolers can try patterns such as red, blue, yellow, red, blue, yellow.

ACTIVITY 08

Storytelling with toys

Use stuffed animals, dolls or toy figures to create a tiny story together.

🧸
BEGIN

Give the character a problem

Maybe the teddy bear lost its hat or the toy car can't find its way home.

💭
ASK

Let your child decide

Ask what should happen next and follow their ideas.

🗣️
LEARNING

Build language naturally

Storytelling gives children opportunities to practise vocabulary, sequencing and communication.

ACTIVITY 09

Water transfer play

During supervised water play, let your child move water between different containers and explore what happens.

💧
POUR

Pour from one container to another

Use cups or containers that are easy for your child to hold.

🥛
COMPARE

Which holds more?

Ask your child which container they think will hold more before testing it.

👐
SKILLS

Practise coordination

Pouring, scooping and transferring help children practise controlled hand movements.

ACTIVITY 10

Scavenger hunt

Give your child a few simple things to find around the house or outside.

🔍
FIND

Something red

Start with easy visual clues and let your child search for something that matches.

🟦
DESCRIBE

Add details

Ask your child to find something blue, small, soft or round.

🏃
MOVEMENT

Make it active

Let your child walk, crawl or move around while completing the hunt.

ACTIVITY 11

Draw a face

Give your child a piece of paper and ask them to create a face. This simple activity can turn into a conversation about body parts and emotions.

🙂
DRAW

Start with simple shapes

A circle for the head and simple lines or shapes for facial features are enough.

👀
LEARN

Name body parts

Talk about eyes, ears, nose, mouth and other familiar body parts while your child draws.

❤️
EMOTIONS

Add different expressions

Ask what a happy, sad, surprised or sleepy face might look like.

ACTIVITY 12

Everyday sorting

Give your toddler a small collection of safe household objects and let them practise putting similar things together.

🧦
LAUNDRY

Match socks

Matching socks can become a simple game while also giving your child an opportunity to practise visual comparison.

🥄
KITCHEN

Sort safe utensils

With appropriate supervision, let your child group safe items according to type, size or another obvious feature.

🧸
TOYS

Make toy groups

Ask your child to put cars together, animals together or blocks together.

ACTIVITY 13

Sound guessing game

Make familiar sounds and ask your child what they think made the sound.

🐶
ANIMALS

Guess animal sounds

Try familiar animal sounds and let your child guess the animal.

🚗
EVERYDAY

Use familiar sounds

Talk about sounds such as cars, doors, running water or household appliances.

👂
LISTENING

Ask what they heard

Pause during a walk and ask your child to listen carefully and describe the sounds around them.

ACTIVITY 14

Five-minute book challenge

Pick a familiar picture book and turn reading into a conversation rather than simply reading every word on the page.

📖
LOOK

Ask what they see

Let your child point out objects, people, colours and animals they recognize.

❓
THINK

Ask what happens next

Pause before turning the page and let your child make a prediction.

💬
TALK

Connect it to real life

Ask whether your child has ever seen, eaten, touched or done something similar to what appears in the book.

ACTIVITY 15

"I Spy" for toddlers and preschoolers

This classic game can be adjusted to your child's age and turned into a quick learning activity almost anywhere.

👀
YOUNGER TODDLERS

Start with colours

"I spy something blue" is an easy way to practise colour recognition and vocabulary.

🔺
PRESCHOOLERS

Add shapes

Ask your child to find something shaped like a circle, square or triangle.

🔤
OLDER PRESCHOOLERS

Add letters

Ask your child to find something that starts with a particular sound or contains a familiar letter.

THE TAKEAWAY

You don't need to turn your home into a classroom

The best learning activities are often the ones you can repeat easily. A few minutes of talking, counting, sorting, reading, drawing or exploring can become part of your child's everyday routine.

❤️
CONNECTION

Follow their interests

If your child loves animals, use animals for counting, drawing, reading and sorting activities.

🔁
REPETITION

Repeat favourites

Children don't need a completely new activity every day. Repeating familiar activities gives them opportunities to practise and become more confident.

🌱
PROGRESS

Keep expectations flexible

Toddlers and preschoolers develop at different rates. Keep activities playful and age-appropriate rather than turning every activity into a test.
