🎨 TODDLER & PRESCHOOL LEARNING

Easy Learning Activities for Toddlers & Preschoolers

You don't need expensive toys or complicated lesson plans to help your little one learn. These easy activities use simple materials and everyday moments to practise early skills through play.

💗 MomYouNeedThis • Easy At-Home Activities
Learning doesn't have to feel like school.

For toddlers and preschoolers, some of the best learning happens while they are playing, moving, sorting, talking, drawing and exploring.

The goal isn't to keep your child busy with activities all day. It's simply to create little opportunities to practise important skills in a fun and age-appropriate way.

💡 The Quick Answer

If you're looking for easy activities to start with, focus on a few basic learning areas:

🔤

LETTERS

Recognizing letters and hearing their sounds.

🔢

NUMBERS

Counting, matching quantities and noticing numbers.

🎨

CREATIVITY

Drawing, colouring, creating and experimenting.

🧩

THINKING

Sorting, matching, comparing and solving simple problems.

You don't need to practise all of these every day. Follow your child's interest and keep activities short enough that they remain enjoyable.

🔤 Easy Letter Activities

You can introduce letters without sitting down for a formal lesson. Toddlers and preschoolers can start noticing letters through play.

🔤
Letter Hunt

Pick one letter and look for it around the house, on books, packaging, signs or other safe everyday items.

✏️
Trace & Find

Write a large letter on paper and let your child trace it with a finger, crayon or washable marker.

🧲
Letter Matching

Write a few uppercase letters and their lowercase matches on pieces of paper and let your child pair them together.

Keep it simple:

At this age, recognizing a few letters well is more useful than trying to rush through the entire alphabet.

🔢 Easy Number Activities

Numbers are everywhere. Everyday routines can become simple counting games without needing worksheets.

🍎 Count Snacks
🧸 Count Toys
🧦 Match Socks
🪜 Count Steps
🚗 Count Cars
🖍️ Count Crayons

You can also ask simple questions such as:

“Can you give me three blocks?”

This helps connect number words with actual quantities.

🎨 Easy Colour Activities

Colour learning can happen naturally while your child plays and explores.

1
Colour Hunt

Choose a colour and ask your child to find objects around the room that match it.

2
Colour Sorting

Use blocks, toys, socks or other safe objects and sort them into groups by colour.

3
Colour Mixing

If you're using washable paint, let your child experiment with combining different colours and observing what happens.

You don't need to turn every activity into a quiz. Sometimes simply talking about what your child is doing provides plenty of learning.

🔺 Shapes & Matching

Shape recognition can be practised using objects you already have at home.

Circle → Square → Triangle → Rectangle

Look for these shapes together around your home.

  • Plates and cups
  • Doors and windows
  • Books
  • Boxes
  • Signs
  • Toys

For older preschoolers, you can ask questions such as:

“Which shape has three sides?”

🧩 Sorting & Matching Games

Sorting is a simple way to practise early math and thinking skills.

🧦 Sort by Colour
📏 Sort by Size
🔵 Match Shapes
🧸 Match Pairs

Start with only a few objects. As your child understands the activity, you can make the categories more challenging.

Try asking:

“Can you find everything that belongs together?”

✋ Fine Motor Activities

Fine motor activities give children opportunities to practise using their hands and fingers for everyday tasks.

🖍️
Colouring

Give your child paper and age-appropriate crayons or colouring materials and let them experiment.

🧱
Building

Blocks and stacking activities encourage children to grasp, place and combine objects.

📄
Tearing & Gluing

With appropriate supervision, simple paper crafts can encourage hand control and creativity.

🧩
Simple Puzzles

Age-appropriate puzzles encourage children to manipulate pieces and think about how they fit together.

📚 Language & Vocabulary Activities

You don't need flashcards to build vocabulary. Conversations throughout the day can provide constant opportunities to learn new words.

TRY THIS

Instead of simply saying “Look at the dog,” try adding a little more language:

“The big brown dog is running!”

You can also talk about:

  • Colours
  • Sizes
  • Textures
  • Actions
  • Feelings
  • Animals
  • Food
  • What you see outside

Reading the same favourite books repeatedly is also valuable. Your child doesn't need a new book every day to benefit from reading together.

🌱 Everyday Life Is Learning

Some of the best learning activities don't look like activities at all.

🥣

COOKING

Talk about ingredients, colours, quantities and textures.

🧺

LAUNDRY

Match socks, sort colours and talk about clothing.

🛒

SHOPPING

Count items and talk about shapes, colours and categories.

🌳

OUTSIDE

Notice leaves, rocks, animals, sounds and movement.

Your child doesn't need a perfectly prepared learning environment. Ordinary life gives you countless opportunities to talk, count, compare, sort and explore.

🧸 What Should I Do With A Toddler vs. A Preschooler?

The same activity can often be adapted depending on your child's age and development.

TODDLER

Focus on exploring, matching, naming, sorting, simple counting and short activities with lots of hands-on play.

PRESCHOOLER

Add more challenges such as letter sounds, simple patterns, counting larger quantities, tracing and basic problem-solving.

You don't have to make an activity harder just because your child is older. Follow their interest and adjust the challenge when they are ready.

💗 You Don't Need To Entertain Your Child All Day

This is important.

Learning activities are a tool — they aren't supposed to become another thing you have to accomplish perfectly every day.

Play, movement, conversation and everyday life are learning too.

It's completely okay for your child to have unstructured play time. It's also okay for an activity to last five minutes instead of thirty.

If your child loses interest, you can stop. Tomorrow is another opportunity.

🌈 A Simple Learning Day

If you want an easy routine, you could simply sprinkle small learning moments throughout the day.

Breakfast → Count & Talk → Play → Read → Create

For example:

  • Count pieces of food at breakfast.
  • Look for colours while getting dressed.
  • Read one favourite book.
  • Do a five-minute colouring activity.
  • Sort toys while cleaning up.
  • Count steps while walking.

That's already a lot of learning.

🎨

Learning Can Be Much Simpler Than You Think

Your toddler or preschooler doesn't need a complicated curriculum, expensive educational toys or hours of structured activities.

They need opportunities to talk, move, explore, create, practise and play.

Start with one simple activity. See if they enjoy it. Then follow their curiosity.

The goal isn't to create a perfect little student. It's to make learning feel natural, fun and part of everyday life. 💗
