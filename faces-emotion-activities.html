Faces are a child’s first window into feelings. These printable activities invite kids to draw expressions, match faces to feeling words, and explore how emotions look — building emotional awareness through creativity.

🙂 Learn About Faces & Emotions

Children complete half-drawn faces by adding eyes and mouths to show different feelings, match feeling words to expression cards, and draw their own “happy face” and “sad face.” The playful format helps young learners connect what a feeling looks like on the outside with what it feels like inside.

🔍 Make A Feelings Mirror Game

Sit with a mirror and take turns copying faces from the printable — then try inventing new ones. Ask “what made you feel like that today?” to connect the expression to a real memory. It is a simple, screen-free way to practice emotional awareness together.

💡 Why Faces Help Kids Understand Feelings

Young children read faces long before they can read words. Practicing with illustrated faces gives them a visual anchor for abstract feelings, making it easier to recognize emotions in themselves and in others — a foundation for empathy and social confidence.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What do kids learn from faces and emotions activities?

They learn to connect facial expressions with feeling words, practice drawing emotions, and recognize how feelings look on others. It is a visual, hands-on introduction to emotional literacy for ages 2 to 5.

Are these printables really free?

Yes — every printable on MomYouNeedThis is 100% free to download and print. No signup, no payment, just tap the download button.

My child only draws happy faces. Is that okay?

Completely normal. Start where they are comfortable, then gently introduce other faces through the matching games on the sheet. Over time, and with your example, they will add more feelings to their drawings.
