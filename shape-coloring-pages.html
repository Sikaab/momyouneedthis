Shape Hunt Around Your Home

Printed shapes stick when real ones match them. After a colouring session, walk the house on a shape hunt with the finished page as the checklist:

Circles — a clock, a plate, a lid. Rectangles — the door you just walked through, a book, the TV. Squares — a floor tile, a cracker, a sticky note. Triangles — the hardest hunt: a pizza slice, a folded sandwich cut corner to corner, a hanger.

Call out sides and corners as you find each one — "door: rectangle, four corners" — and the counting from the colouring page becomes the game.

Meet the Shapes: Sides and Corners

Every shape on this page can be introduced the same way: count the sides, count the corners. Fingers do the teaching — trace each edge, tap each corner.

  • Circle — no sides, no corners: one round line that never ends. A wheel, a clock, the sun.
  • Square — 4 sides, all the same length, and 4 corners. A window, a cracker, a floor tile.
  • Triangle — 3 sides, 3 corners: the smallest team that can hold a shape up. A roof, a slice of pizza.
  • Rectangle — 4 corners like a square, but two sides are long and two are short. A door, a book, a bus.
  • Oval — a circle stretched long: still no corners. An egg, a balloon, a mirror.
  • Star — 5 points that take turns pointing out and in. The shape of "you did it".
  • Heart — two round bumps on top and one point at the bottom, with no straight sides at all.

And the sheet on this page mixes them all — circle, square, triangle, star and more in one design, for when naming them one at a time feels easy.
