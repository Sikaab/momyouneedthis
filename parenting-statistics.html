🔎 How We Research

A statistic is only worth sharing if you can see where it came from. That is the standard every report in this hub is built to — whether the numbers come from public datasets and published research or from search data we measured ourselves.

  • Data and year, stated: every report names the data behind it and the year that data covers.
  • Method, on the page: how the figures were gathered and calculated is explained in the report itself, not hidden.
  • Sources you can check: each report lists its sources, so you can go back to the original numbers.
  • Refreshed, not frozen: figures are updated as new editions of the underlying data publish.

If we cannot verify a number, we do not publish it. And when two sources disagree, the report says so instead of quietly picking the bigger figure.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Parenting Statistics hub?

It is the home of MomYouNeedThis's research reports — data-led pages on childcare costs, reading aloud and what moms search for. Each report is built from named public data or from search data we measured ourselves.

Where does the data in these reports come from?

Every report states its data, the year the data covers, its method and its sources on the page, so you can see exactly where each number came from before you use it.

How often are the reports updated?

Figures are refreshed as new editions of the underlying data publish. Each report shows the year its data covers, so you can always see how current the numbers are.

Can I cite or share these statistics?

Yes — that is what they are here for. Please link back to the report page you used, so your readers can see the full data, method and sources behind the number.
