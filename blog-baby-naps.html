How Many Naps Does My Baby Need?

Baby naps can feel like a moving target. One day your baby takes beautiful naps and the next day everything seems to fall apart. Here's how naps generally change as babies grow and how to tell when your baby's daytime sleep may need a little adjustment.

💗 MomYouNeedThis • Practical Parenting Guide
First, remember: there isn't one perfect nap schedule. 😴

Babies have different sleep needs, and their nap patterns naturally change as they grow.

Age can give you a useful starting point, but your baby's total sleep, mood, awake periods and nighttime sleep matter too.

Think of a nap schedule as a flexible framework rather than a strict set of times that your baby has to follow every day.

💡 The Quick Answer

The number of naps a baby needs depends largely on their age and total sleep needs.

😴
Younger babies usually need more naps.

As babies grow, they generally transition from several naps throughout the day toward fewer, longer periods of daytime sleep.

A baby may also temporarily nap more or less because of growth, development, illness, travel, changes in routine or simply having a different day.

🌙 How Many Naps Do Babies Usually Take?

There is a lot of individual variation, but these broad patterns can help you understand how daytime sleep often changes during the first year.

👶 Young infants

Often sleep in several periods throughout the day.

🌤️ Around 4–6 months

Many babies begin settling into a more predictable nap pattern.

😴 Around 6–9 months

Many babies are taking about two to three naps.

🌙 Around 9–12 months

Many babies move toward two daytime naps.

☀️ Around 12–18 months

Many toddlers gradually transition toward one main nap.

🧸 After the transition

One daytime nap may continue for quite some time.

These are general patterns, not requirements. Some babies transition earlier and others later.

Look at your baby, not just the number.

The number of naps is only one part of the picture. Total sleep over 24 hours, your baby's behaviour and the timing of sleep all matter.

👶 What About Newborn Naps?

Newborn sleep is usually much less predictable than the sleep of an older baby.

Newborns often sleep frequently throughout the day and night rather than following a neat nap schedule.

Instead of trying to create a strict schedule, it is generally more useful to focus on feeding, safe sleep and responding to your newborn's sleep cues.

Their sleep patterns can change considerably from one day to another.

🌤️ Around 3–4 Months

Around this stage, some babies begin developing a more recognizable pattern of daytime sleep.

You may notice:

  • More predictable periods of sleep
  • Longer stretches of awake time
  • Naps beginning to fall into a more recognizable rhythm
  • Some naps becoming shorter than others
  • Different sleep needs from one day to another

This is still a period of significant development, so consistency can be helpful without expecting every nap to happen at exactly the same time.

😴 Around 4–6 Months

Many babies around this age are beginning to settle into a daytime pattern that includes several naps.

A common pattern is roughly three naps, although some babies may take more or fewer depending on the length and quality of their naps.

☀️
Don't judge the schedule by nap count alone.

A baby taking three short naps may have a very different daytime sleep pattern from a baby taking three longer naps.

🌙 Around 6–9 Months

Many babies in this age range begin moving toward two or three naps.

You may start noticing that one nap becomes harder to fit into the day, particularly when your baby's awake periods become longer.

This can be one of the first signs that a nap transition may be approaching.

But a few difficult naps do not necessarily mean your baby is ready to drop one.

🌤️ Around 9–12 Months

Many babies at this stage are taking two daytime naps.

A typical rhythm may include:

  • A morning nap
  • An afternoon nap
  • A longer period of awake time before bedtime

Some babies may occasionally need an extra short nap, especially after a poor night's sleep or an unusually active day.

Two naps doesn't mean every day will look identical.

Travel, illness, developmental changes and short naps can all affect the day's schedule.

☀️ Around 12–18 Months

This is a common period for the transition from two naps toward one.

The transition can take time.

You may notice:

  • One nap becoming difficult
  • Your child refusing the morning nap
  • The afternoon nap moving later
  • Bedtime becoming harder when daytime sleep is too late
  • Some days requiring two naps while other days work better with one

You don't necessarily need to eliminate a nap immediately. A gradual transition can be easier for some children.

🧠 How Do I Know If My Baby Needs Another Nap?

Sometimes it is difficult to tell whether your baby is ready for another nap or simply having a difficult day.

Signs that your baby may still need another period of daytime sleep can include:

  • Becoming very tired before bedtime
  • Falling asleep unexpectedly late in the day
  • Becoming increasingly fussy as the day continues
  • Struggling to stay comfortably awake until bedtime
  • Having consistently long awake periods before the next sleep

One difficult day isn't enough to establish a pattern. Look at what is happening consistently over several days.

🔄 How Do I Know If My Baby Is Ready To Drop A Nap?

Nap transitions are often gradual rather than happening overnight.

You might suspect your baby is ready for fewer naps if they consistently:

  • Refuse one particular nap
  • Take a long time to fall asleep for that nap
  • Have a very long period of alertness during the nap
  • Still seem well rested without that nap
  • Begin having bedtime difficulties because the final nap is too late
Consistency matters.

If your baby refuses a nap once or twice, that doesn't necessarily mean they are ready to drop it. Look for a repeated pattern.

⏱️ What If My Baby Only Takes Short Naps?

Short naps are incredibly common and can be frustrating.

A short nap doesn't automatically mean something is wrong.

Babies may wake after one sleep cycle, have difficulty connecting sleep cycles or simply have a day when they need less sleep.

Instead of immediately changing the entire schedule, consider:

  • Whether your baby is otherwise happy and rested
  • How long they were awake before the nap
  • Whether the nap timing is consistent
  • How much total sleep they are getting over 24 hours
  • Whether nighttime sleep has changed

🌙 Can Naps Affect Nighttime Sleep?

Yes. Daytime sleep and nighttime sleep are connected.

Too little daytime sleep can sometimes contribute to overtiredness, while too much or poorly timed daytime sleep may reduce the amount of sleep pressure available at bedtime.

Morning → Nap 1 → Awake time → Nap 2 → Bedtime → Night sleep

That's why changing one nap can sometimes affect bedtime or nighttime waking.

🌆 What If The Last Nap Is Too Late?

A late nap can sometimes make bedtime more difficult because your baby has had less time awake before nighttime sleep.

If bedtime suddenly becomes much harder, consider whether the final nap has shifted later or become longer.

However, don't assume every late nap is a problem. A baby who is genuinely tired may need that sleep.

😴 Can Too Few Naps Make My Baby Overtired?

They can.

If a baby stays awake longer than they can comfortably handle, they may become increasingly fussy and have a harder time settling.

You might notice:

  • More crying before sleep
  • Difficulty settling
  • Short naps
  • Waking shortly after falling asleep
  • More difficult evenings

This is one reason it can be helpful to look at the entire day's sleep rather than simply trying to push bedtime later.

📅 Should I Put My Baby On A Strict Nap Schedule?

Some families find predictable nap times helpful, while others prefer a more flexible routine.

For many babies, a combination works well: use a general rhythm while allowing the exact timing to change depending on when your baby woke, how long the previous nap lasted and how they are doing that day.

⏰
Think rhythm, not perfection.

A predictable sequence can be useful without requiring every nap to happen at exactly the same minute.

What Should I Do If Naps Are Suddenly A Mess?

When naps change suddenly, start simple.

1
Check the basics.

Consider hunger, illness, discomfort and the sleep environment.

2
Look at the previous night's sleep.

A poor night can change daytime sleep needs.

3
Look at the entire day's pattern.

Consider wake-up time, naps, awake periods and bedtime together.

4
Look for a repeated pattern.

One difficult nap doesn't necessarily mean your baby's schedule needs to change.

5
Make one small adjustment.

If the pattern continues, try changing one part of the schedule rather than changing everything at once.

What Should I Avoid Doing?

❌ Don't Force A Nap Just Because The Clock Says So

A schedule is a guide. Your baby's needs can vary from day to day.

❌ Don't Drop A Nap After One Bad Day

Nap transitions usually become clearer when you look at patterns over time.

❌ Don't Ignore Overtiredness

Pushing through a tired baby simply to protect a schedule can sometimes make settling harder.

❌ Don't Change Everything At Once

Small adjustments make it easier to understand what is actually helping.

🌙 A Quick Nap Check

If naps aren't working well, ask yourself:

☀️ When did baby wake?
😴 How long was the last nap?
⏰ When is the next sleep?
🌙 What time is bedtime?
🍼 Has feeding changed?
🤒 Any signs of discomfort?

The Most Important Thing: Look At The Whole 24 Hours

It can be tempting to judge a nap based only on whether your baby slept for a certain amount of time.

But the bigger picture matters.

Wake-up → Feeding → Awake time → Naps → Bedtime → Night sleep

Looking at the full day can help you understand whether your baby may need more daytime sleep, less daytime sleep or simply a little flexibility.

When Should I Talk To My Baby's Doctor?

Nap changes are often normal, but sleep can sometimes change because a baby is uncomfortable or unwell.

Talk with your baby's healthcare professional if you are concerned about your baby's sleep, feeding, growth, development or overall health.

If your baby is having significant breathing difficulty, is unusually difficult to wake, is not feeding normally or has other concerning symptoms, seek appropriate medical attention promptly.

You know your baby's normal better than anyone.

If something feels noticeably different and you're worried, it's okay to ask for professional guidance.

😴

Baby Naps Don't Have To Be Perfect

One of the hardest parts of baby sleep is that naps can change just as soon as you think you've figured them out.

That's normal.

Your baby's sleep needs change with growth, development, nighttime sleep and their individual temperament.

“Think about the whole day before deciding that a nap is the problem.”

Use age-based information as a starting point, watch your baby's patterns and make small adjustments when they seem necessary.

You don't need perfect naps to be doing a great job. 💗
