👂 Why Practise Letter Sounds?

Learning to connect a written letter with the sound it represents is the heart of early phonics. Lowercase letters carry that practice furthest: nearly every word in a storybook is lowercase, so the sounds your child attaches to these small shapes are the ones they will use on every page they ever read. That connection prepares them for blending sounds into words when reading begins.

📖 Why Small Letters Do The Heavy Lifting

Open any storybook and count: almost every word on the page is written in small letters. Capitals appear once a sentence; lowercase letters do all the rest of the work. That is why sound practice with small letters pays off so quickly — every sound your child attaches to a lowercase shape is a sound they will use on every line of every book.

It also explains the classic mix-ups. Small letters are more alike than big ones — b and d are mirror twins, and a, e and o are all little round shapes — so lowercase practice doubles as looking practice. The sound pages in the grid above give each small letter its own page, so your child can master one shape and one sound at a time.
