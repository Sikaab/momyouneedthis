✏️ How to Draw a Dog: Step by Step

One step per box — draw each new part in a different color so kids can see exactly what changed.

Step 1: The Head

Draw a big circle in the middle of your paper. This round shape becomes your dog's head.

Step 2: Floppy Ears

Draw two long rounded ears hanging down on each side of the circle, like upside-down U shapes.

Step 3: The Happy Face

Draw two dot eyes, a round nose, and a big smile with a tongue sticking out one side.

Step 4: The Body

Draw a small sideways oval below the head for the body — smaller than the head circle. Let it overlap the head a little.

Step 5: The Legs

Add four short rectangle legs under the body — two in front, two peeking out behind.

Step 6: Tail and Spots

Draw a waggy tail curving up at the back, add a few spots, and color your pup in!

💡 Dog Drawing Tips for Parents

The ears are the likeliest place for a wobble: one long floppy ear and one short one. Treat that as a dog caught mid-headshake, not as a mistake. If your child wants the ears closer in length, demonstrate on scrap paper — never on their drawing — how the shorter ear can be lengthened downward. Ears can grow, and the picture stays theirs.

Body size gives your child a choice, too. A body oval that is bigger than the head reads as a grown-up dog; a smaller body reads as a puppy. Let them declare which one they drew instead of correcting the proportions for them. Then put the finished dog on display — a drawing that gets a place where everyone can see it is one a child will want to follow with another.

🐶 Dog Drawing Step by Step: Fixes for Wobbly Dogs

Every dog drawing step by step wobbles in the same three places, and all three are fixable without starting over. First, the ears: one long ear and one short ear is the most common complaint, but floppy ears are forgiving — a dog mid-headshake has uneven ears in real life too. If it truly bothers your child, lengthen the short ear downward; ears can always grow, never shrink. Second, the body: if the oval comes out bigger than the head, the dog reads as a grown-up; smaller, and it reads as a puppy. Both are correct dogs — let your child declare which one they drew.

The legs are the real trap. Four legs of different lengths leave the dog floating or tipping, so draw a ground line under the body and stand every leg on it. Short and equal beats long and wobbly. If a leg lands in the wrong spot entirely, cover it with a spot — step 6 adds spots anyway, and a well-placed spot hides a multitude of lines.

The tail finishes the personality: a tail curving up means a happy dog, and two short wag lines beside it make the whole picture move. That is the quiet strength of a dog drawing step by step — each fix is one small line, added where the picture already is, so a wobbly first dog still ends up on the fridge. Most kids draw the second dog from memory, wobbles and all, and it comes out better.
