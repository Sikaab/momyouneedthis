Signs your toddler is ready for potty training

Starting before these signs appear usually makes training take longer, not shorter. Look for most of these first:

  • Stays dry for 2 or more hours at a time
  • Tells you when their diaper is wet or dirty
  • Can pull pants up and down on their own
  • Shows interest in the toilet or bathroom
  • Can follow simple instructions
  • Wants to do things "like a big kid"

Potty training questions, answered

When should I start potty training?

Most children show signs of readiness between 18 months and 3 years, with the average around 2 to 2.5 years. Watch for readiness signs — like staying dry for 2+ hours and showing interest in the bathroom — rather than starting by the calendar.

Do reward charts actually work for potty training?

For many toddlers, yes. A visual chart makes progress feel real, and small rewards keep motivation high through the tricky middle days. Personalizing the chart with your child's name and favorite theme makes it even more exciting for them.

How long does potty training usually take?

It varies widely — anywhere from a few days to a few months. Most children get the hang of daytime training within 3 to 6 months once they are truly ready. Starting before readiness signs appear usually makes it take longer, not shorter.
