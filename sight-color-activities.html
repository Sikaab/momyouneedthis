👁️ How Your Eyes See Color

Seeing is catching light. Light from the sun or a lamp bounces off everything — off a red apple, most of the light that bounces is the red part, and that is the part your eyes catch. The apple is not glowing; it is reflecting. That is why colors disappear in the dark: no light, nothing to catch. A “red” apple at midnight is a gray apple until the light returns.

Inside the eye, the back wall (the retina) is lined with detectors. Some work in dim light but see only shades of gray — they run the night shift. Others, the cones, need brighter light and report color. There are three kinds of cones, tuned roughly to red, green and blue, and every color your child has ever named is a mixture of their three reports.

That is also why the rainbow matters to this page: a rainbow is sunlight split into its full report — every color the cones can catch, laid out in a stripe. And it explains the games here: I-spy and color hunts are training the catching system — speed, accuracy, and the naming that makes a seen color a known one. A child who can find “something turquoise” in a messy room has a visual system working like a search engine.
