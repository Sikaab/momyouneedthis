✏️ How to Draw a Bird: Step by Step

One step per box — draw each new part in a different color so kids can see exactly what changed.

Step 1: The Body

Draw a plump oval tilted slightly, like an egg leaning to one side. This is the bird's body.

Step 2: The Head

Draw a smaller circle at the top of the oval, overlapping it, for the head.

Step 3: The Beak

Draw a small triangle on the front of the head for the beak.

Step 4: The Wing

Draw a curved leaf shape on the side of the body for the wing.

Step 5: Tail and Legs

Add a fan-shaped tail at the back and two thin stick legs underneath.

Step 6: Face and Color

Add a dot eye, then color your bird — blue jay, red robin, or any color you dream up!

🐦 Birds in the Real World

Birds are the only animals with feathers, and feathers do three jobs at once: flying, warmth, and showing off. Not every bird flies — penguins swim, ostriches run faster than a horse, and the kiwi shuffles around at night — but every bird hatches from an egg and starts life smaller than its mother's foot. There are about ten thousand kinds of birds, from the bee hummingbird, smaller than a thumb, to the ostrich, taller than a grown-up.

Birds are also musicians: the dawn chorus your child hears in spring is mostly birds announcing themselves. Hummingbirds can fly backward, owls fly in near silence, and many backyard birds remember exactly which gardens have feeders. Ask your child which bird theirs is — and if it is perched, what it is watching so patiently.

What You'll Need to Draw a Bird

Blue, yellow, or red for the body — let your kid pick their favorite bird color. Black for the outlines, orange or yellow for the beak. The wing is the hardest part, so a crayon with a fine tip helps. Plain white paper is best; the bird's simple shapes get lost on busy backgrounds.

What They're Learning

This bird is built from just two shapes: an oval body and a circle head. That simplicity is the lesson — complex things break down into easy parts. The beak (a triangle) adds a third shape, and positioning the wing correctly on the body works on understanding where things sit in space relative to each other.

Make It Yours

Turn it into different birds: a long beak makes a toucan, a crest on the head makes a cardinal, webbed feet make a duck. Kids can draw a nest with eggs under their bird, add a worm in the beak, or create a whole flock at different sizes. Drawing the bird perched on a branch adds a nice extra challenge. Or skip the branch, spread both wings wide and angled up, and fan the tail — that is how to draw birds in flight, and a few clouds underneath sell the height.

A dove is this page's bird dressed for peace: soft white or pale gray all over, a smaller, finer beak than the backyard original, and smooth, unruffled wings. 🦜 Parrot Bird Drawing

A parrot is the bird shape after a trip to the paint box, but the beak comes first: big, deep, and hooked downward, strong enough to crack nuts — draw it oversized on purpose. 🦅 How to Draw Birds Flying

Once the sitting bird feels easy, birds how to draw in flight is the natural next step. A flying bird is the same bird re-posed. Tip the body forward, take the wings off the sides, and stretch them into two long arcs that meet at the back — from the front they make a wide, shallow W; from the side, a boomerang. The legs disappear into the body, the tail fans open, and one or two curved lines underneath suggest the air doing the lifting.

The fastest practice is a sky strip: three small birds in a row, wings at different heights of the flap — up, middle, down — like frames of a cartoon. Kids grasp flight from that strip faster than from any single pose.

🪹 Bird Nest Drawing

A nest is a drawing of collected things: a rough bowl built from crisscrossed twig lines, messy on purpose, with all the tidiness saved for two or three smooth eggs resting inside. Speckle the eggs lightly. Tuck the bowl onto a branch, and perch the parent bird beside it at small scale — a nest reads as a nest when something small is waiting in it.

🔥 How to Draw Phoenix Bird

A phoenix is a bird built from a campfire's palette: scarlet, orange, and gold, with the tail stretched into three or four long ribbon feathers that curl like flames. Give it a crest of flame shapes on the head, scatter ember dots around the wings, and let the colors run hot at the body and cooler at the feather tips, like real fire.

The legend says the phoenix rises again from its own ashes — which makes it the most encouraging subject a young artist can pick. On this page, mistakes are not failures; they are plot.

🐤 Cute Bird Drawings Easy

A bird drawing easy enough for little hands starts cute: bigger head, rounder body, larger eyes. Cute is a ratio, not a species: head bigger, body rounder, eyes larger and set lower on the face, beak shrunk to a nub. Add pastel colors and one blush dot per cheek and the transformation is complete. The formula works on every bird your child can draw — a genuinely cute eagle is an achievement — and it is the fastest style upgrade available to a beginner.

🐦 Small Bird Drawing

A small bird is the four-shape shortcut: one thumb-sized oval, one circle, one triangle beak, two stick legs. Two minutes, done. Small birds are made for margins, bookmarks, gift tags, and the corner of a letter to Grandma.

Drawn in a row of three or four at slightly different sizes, sitting on one long line, they become a flock on a wire — a complete picture built from almost nothing, and a lovely first 'series' for a young artist.

✏️ Bird Drawing Outline

An outline drawing stops before the details: the oval body and the circle head in light pencil, a wedge for the beak, and nothing else. It is the plan version of the bird — useful for laying out a bigger picture, for tracing practice, and for younger kids who enjoy the shapes but not yet the steps.

Press lightly. An outline you can barely see is an outline you can adjust forever, and learning to work light is the habit that separates relaxed drawers from eraser-dependent ones.
