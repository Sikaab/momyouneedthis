🔎 Say The Words Together

Before a single crayon moves, tour the page and say every picture's name together. Keep cat in mind as the reference: if a name opens with the same little cough, it is a coloring candidate; if it opens with a hiss, a hum or a pop instead, it waits. 🎨 Which Pictures Earn Their Color? The C Rule

Cat, car and cake earn their color on this page because they open with a cough — /k/, from the back of the throat. That is the whole rule. A picture of something sweet that is not a cake does not get colored for being sweet; the ears, not the eyes, hand out the crayons here.

C brings a complication no other letter in this set has: a second sound. In city and circle, c hisses like s. If a hiss-starting picture appears, it raises the best question of the page — same letter, different sound. This page colors the cough; the hiss belongs to a later lesson, and noticing it now is ahead of schedule.

When every cough-picture is colored, read the finished gallery aloud as a cough chorus — c-c-cat, c-c-car, c-c-cake. Then let your child color the leftovers in any shade they like: the listening is done, and the page can end as a treat.
