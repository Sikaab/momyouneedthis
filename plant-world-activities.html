🌍 The Biggest, Smallest And Strangest Plants

The biggest: coast redwoods in California are the tallest living things on Earth — the tallest known is about 116 metres, higher than a 25-story building, and it was already growing a thousand years ago. The heaviest single tree, a giant sequoia nicknamed General Sherman, weighs about as much as ten blue whales.

The smallest: duckweed plants can be smaller than a sesame seed — a whole plant, with a leaf and a root, floating on a pond. Mosses never grow tall at all, and have carpeted the land since long before flowers existed.

The strangest solutions: a cactus wears spines instead of leaves to keep its stored water safe in the desert. A water lily floats flat leaves like rafts and breathes through their tops. A Venus flytrap, living in soil too poor to feed it, eats insects instead — a plant that hunts.

Why the tour matters: every one of these plants is doing the same four jobs — drinking, eating sunlight, standing up, making seeds — with completely different equipment. That is the deepest pattern in nature, and a child who has seen a cactus beside a water lily has already met it.
