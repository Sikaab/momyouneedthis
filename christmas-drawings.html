🎨 Why Christmas Drawings Are Perfect for Kids

Christmas shapes are some of the easiest drawings in the world — and kids already love them. A snowman is three circles. An ornament is one circle with a cap. A Christmas tree is three stacked triangles. There's no intimidating blank page because every subject starts with a shape they already know.

Christmas drawings also make instant gifts and decorations. A hand-drawn Santa taped to the fridge, a reindeer on a homemade card, a snowman on the classroom wall — kids see their work displayed, which is the biggest motivator to draw again. Unlike a worksheet that gets tossed, a Christmas drawing becomes part of the holiday.

And the timing is perfect. December is full of waiting — waiting for school to end, waiting for presents, waiting for the big day. A Christmas drawing tutorial turns that restless energy into 15 minutes of calm focus. Print all six, and you have a week of December activities that cost nothing.

✏️ Christmas Drawing Tips for Parents

  • Start with the snowman or ornament. Both are built from circles — the easiest shape. A quick win builds confidence for the harder ones.
  • Let them pick the colors. A blue Santa is still a Santa. A purple reindeer is still a reindeer. The goal is a drawing they're proud of, not a perfect one.
  • Don't fix their drawing. If they ask for help, draw on a separate piece of paper, not on theirs. Kids who feel ownership come back for the next one; kids who feel corrected stop trying.
  • Display the finished drawings. Tape them to the fridge, the window, or a string across the room. A gallery wall of their own Christmas drawings beats any store-bought decoration.
  • Pair drawing with coloring. After they finish a drawing tutorial, hand them a Christmas coloring page or a paint by numbers sheet to keep the calm going.

🎅 How to Make Christmas Drawings Even More Fun

Christmas drawings work best when they're part of a bigger holiday activity. Turn the finished drawings into a paper chain — cut out each drawing, punch a hole at the top, and hang them from a string across the window. A chain of six Christmas drawings becomes a decoration kids made themselves, which feels completely different from something bought at a store.

Another idea: make Christmas cards. Fold a piece of construction paper in half, glue the drawing on the front, and let them write (or dictate) a message inside. Grandparents love these. A hand-drawn Santa on a homemade card beats a store-bought card every single time, and it gives kids a reason to draw carefully — they're making something for someone they love.

For classroom or group settings, print all six tutorials and set up a Christmas drawing station. Each child picks their favorite, follows the 6 steps, and adds their drawing to a class display. The variety is the point — a wall with snowmen next to reindeer next to gingerbread men looks like a real Christmas scene, and every child contributed.

Don't forget the paint by numbers sheets. After they finish a Christmas drawing tutorial, the Christmas paint by numbers sheets use the same characters — Santa, reindeer, snowman — in a different format. Drawing first, then color-by-number, gives kids two ways to enjoy the same Christmas characters without repeating the same activity.

🎁 Christmas Drawings as Gifts and Decorations

The best thing about Christmas drawings is that they're finished products, not just activities. A snowman drawing taped to the fridge is a decoration. A reindeer drawing glued to a card is a gift. A Christmas tree drawing framed and wrapped is a present grandparents will keep. When kids realize their drawings have a purpose beyond the table, they draw with more care — and they want to draw more.

Here's a simple gift idea: have your child draw all six Christmas drawings, one per day in the week before Christmas. On Christmas Eve, bind them together with a stapler or ribbon into a little book. Write "Merry Christmas" on the cover. It costs nothing, it took a week of calm afternoons, and it's the most personal gift they'll give all year.

For decorations, the ornament and snowman drawings work especially well because they're simple shapes. Cut them out, punch a hole, thread ribbon through, and hang them on the tree. Handmade ornaments made from your child's own Christmas drawings are the ones that stay on the tree year after year.
