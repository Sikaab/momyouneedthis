🧩 Sorting By The B Sound

B is the popping letter, and popping is what this sort is listening for. Bear, ball and banana open with a burst of air from the lips — say them and you can feel it. Cards whose names start with that same pop belong in the B pile; everything else, however round or furry or yellow it looks, belongs in the other one.

The trap in this sort is pictures that feel like b-words without sounding like them. A picture can show something soft and brown and still start with a completely different sound. The ears decide: stretch the first sound, and if the lips do not pop, the card does not sort into B.

B also hides at the ends of words — tub, web, crab — and in the middle of rabbit. After the sort, ask your child to find a b somewhere else in a sorted word; it is a good reminder that beginning sounds are only where this game starts.

What literacy skills does this practice?

Beginning sounds, listening discrimination, and sorting — the thinking skill underneath early math as well as reading. This sort trains one decision in particular: does this word start with /b/, or not? The hardest cards are the near-misses, like its quiet twin p — same lip pop, no voice, and those are exactly where the learning happens.
